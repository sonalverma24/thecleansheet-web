# PREAMBLE

**The Standards Council of The Clean Sheet, issuing this Standard:**

**(1)** Recognising that cosmetic safety is not sufficiently guaranteed by compliance with the law of any single jurisdiction alone, and that Indian consumers are served by products manufactured and marketed across many regulatory regimes operating under varying levels of consumer protection;

**(2)** Recognising further that no single global regulation currently combines all dimensions of cosmetic safety — ingredient prohibition, ingredient restriction, safety assessment methodology, manufacturing quality, claims integrity, and contaminant control — into a unified, independently verifiable standard;

**(3)** Noting that the European Union Cosmetics Regulation (EC) No 1223/2009 and its amendments represent the world's most comprehensive and consistently enforced ingredient safety framework; that the India Cosmetics Rules 2020 establish the legal baseline applicable to all products sold in India; that the US FDA under 21 CFR and the Modernization of Cosmetics Regulation Act 2022 (MoCRA) have introduced new systematic requirements; that Health Canada's Cosmetic Ingredient Hotlist and Natural Health Products Ingredients Database (NHPID) identify substances of concern with particular relevance to botanical and natural formulations; that the Korea Ministry of Food and Drug Safety (MFDS) maintains one of the world's most rigorous positive-list systems; that Australia's AICIS and TGA frameworks regulate cosmetic chemicals and therapeutic cosmetics respectively; that the European Chemicals Agency (ECHA) Substances of Very High Concern (SVHC) Candidate List identifies substances with the most serious hazard profiles; and that the International Agency for Research on Cancer (IARC) provides the scientific gold standard for carcinogen classification;

**(4)** Having reviewed in full each of the frameworks listed in Article 33, and having determined that where those frameworks differ, the most protective requirement serves consumer interests and shall apply;

**(5)** Determining that a single, published, citable standard is necessary to provide brands and consumers with a transparent, consistent, and auditable benchmark;

**Hereby publishes The Clean Sheet Cosmetics Standard (TCCS), Version 1.0 (2026), as follows:**

# CHAPTER I — SCOPE AND GENERAL PROVISIONS

---

## Article 1: Subject Matter and Scope

**1.1** This Standard establishes the requirements a cosmetic product must meet to be certified under The Clean Sheet certification system.

**1.2** This Standard applies to all products that meet the definition of a cosmetic product under Article 2(1) and that are submitted by a brand for TCS certification.

**1.3** Product categories covered include but are not limited to: face cleansers, toners, moisturisers, serums, eye creams, lip products, exfoliants, facial masks, sunscreens, body lotions, body washes, hand creams, deodorants, hair shampoos, conditioners, hair serums, hair oils, scalp treatments, baby skincare, baby haircare, colour cosmetics (foundation, lipstick, eyeshadow, mascara, kajal, blush, highlighter, primer), and nail care.

**1.4** This Standard does not apply to pharmaceutical drugs; products classified as Ayurvedic, Siddha, or Unani drugs requiring an AYUSH drug licence; medical devices as defined under the Medical Devices Rules 2017; or therapeutic goods as classified under Australian therapeutic goods legislation (where such products require a separate TGA registration — this Standard cannot serve as a substitute for such registration).

**1.5** Where a product straddles the boundary between a cosmetic and a drug in any declared target market, the brand is responsible for obtaining the required drug regulatory approval for that market. TCS certification covers only the cosmetic aspects of such a product and shall not be used to imply drug regulatory approval.

---

## Article 2: Definitions

For the purposes of this Standard:

**2.1 "Cosmetic product"** means any substance or mixture intended to be placed in contact with the external parts of the human body (epidermis, hair system, nails, lips, and external genital organs) or with the teeth and the mucous membranes of the oral cavity, with a view exclusively or mainly to cleaning them, perfuming them, changing their appearance, protecting them, keeping them in good condition, or correcting body odours (consistent with EU Cosmetics Regulation Article 2(1)(a) and India Cosmetics Rules 2020 Rule 3(i)).

**2.2 "Brand"** means the legal entity placing the product on the market — whether as manufacturer, importer, or distributor — which is responsible for the product's compliance with this Standard.

**2.3 "TCS certification"** means The Clean Sheet Cosmetics Standard certification, issued upon successful evaluation of a product dossier against this Standard.

**2.4 "Leave-on product"** means a product intended to remain on skin or hair after application without rinsing. Examples: moisturisers, serums, sunscreens, eye creams, hair serums, leave-in conditioners, body lotions.

**2.5 "Rinse-off product"** means a product intended to be applied to the skin or hair and subsequently washed off. Examples: shampoos, cleansers, body washes, rinse-off conditioners.

**2.6 "INCI name"** means the ingredient name assigned under the International Nomenclature of Cosmetic Ingredients, as maintained by the Personal Care Products Council (PCPC). Where no INCI name exists, the IUPAC name or established scientific nomenclature shall be used.

**2.7 "CMR substance"** means a substance classified as Carcinogenic, Mutagenic, or toxic to Reproduction under the United Nations Globally Harmonised System (GHS) or EU CLP Regulation 1272/2008. Categories 1A and 1B represent the highest hazard.

**2.8 "SVHC"** means a Substance of Very High Concern as identified by the European Chemicals Agency (ECHA) under REACH Regulation (EC) 1907/2006, and included on the SVHC Candidate List.

**2.9 "Nanomaterial"** means an insoluble or biopersistent material with one or more external dimensions or internal structures in the 1–100 nanometre range, consistent with the definition in EU Cosmetics Regulation Article 2(1)(k).

**2.10 "Margin of Safety (MoS)"** means the ratio NOAEL ÷ Systemic Exposure Dose (SED). A minimum MoS of 100 is required for general adult-use products under this Standard.

**2.11 "NOAEL"** means the No Observed Adverse Effect Level — the highest dose tested at which no toxicologically significant adverse effect is observed.

**2.12 "SED (Systemic Exposure Dose)"** means the estimated daily systemic absorption, in mg per kg body weight per day, from all relevant cosmetic products combined, calculated per SCCS methodology.

**2.13 "Formaldehyde releaser"** means an ingredient that releases formaldehyde under normal conditions of storage or use. The following substances are designated TCS formaldehyde releasers: DMDM Hydantoin, Quaternium-15, Imidazolidinyl Urea, Diazolidinyl Urea, Bronopol (2-Bromo-2-Nitropropane-1,3-diol), Sodium Hydroxymethylglycinate, 5-Bromo-5-Nitro-1,3-Dioxane, Paraformaldehyde, Methenamine (Hexamethylenetetramine), Glyoxal.

**2.14 "Safety assessor"** means a person with the qualifications and competence to conduct a cosmetic product safety assessment consistent with the SCCS Notes of Guidance (current revision) and the requirements of Article 13.

**2.15 "Product dossier" (or Product Information File, PIF)** means the complete technical documentation for a product, including full formulation with concentrations, safety assessment, manufacturing documentation, test results, and claims evidence.

**2.16 "Analytical detection threshold"** means the concentration below which a substance's presence in a finished product is not considered actionable under this Standard. Unless stated otherwise, the TCS analytical detection threshold is 0.01% w/w or the method's established limit of detection, whichever is lower.

---

## Article 3: The Governing Hierarchy

**3.1** This Standard is not a substitute for compliance with the law of any declared target market. Brands are independently responsible for all registrations, licences, notifications, and other regulatory requirements in each market where a product is sold.

**3.2** Where requirements of this Standard are stricter than the law of a target market, this Standard's requirements govern for TCS certification purposes.

**3.3** Where the law of a target market is stricter than this Standard, the law governs and TCS will not certify a product that violates applicable law.

**3.4** In all cases, TCS applies the most protective requirement from among all applicable frameworks — its own and all source regulatory frameworks listed in Article 33. This is the Strictest Standard Principle, further articulated in Article 34.

# CHAPTER II — INGREDIENT SAFETY REQUIREMENTS

---

## Article 4: General Cosmetic Safety Obligation

**4.1** A cosmetic product shall be safe for human health when used under normal or reasonably foreseeable conditions of use, taking into account the product's presentation, labelling, instructions for use and disposal, and any other indication or information by the brand.

**4.2** Safety is a property of the complete formulation as a whole — not a simple aggregate of individual ingredient safety profiles. Concentration, pH, delivery system, ingredient interactions, packaging, and pattern of use all determine what a product does in and to the body. No product shall be certified solely on the basis of individual ingredient status without a formulation-level safety assessment.

**4.3** The safety obligation extends to all populations for which a product is marketed. Products marketed for infants or children up to 36 months, pregnant individuals, or persons with sensitive skin are subject to the additional population-specific requirements of this Standard.

---

## Article 5: Prohibited Substances

**5.1** No product certified under this Standard shall contain any substance listed in Annex I of this Standard (the TCS Prohibited Substances List) at any concentration above the TCS analytical detection threshold.

**5.2** Annex I is TCS's own independent determination of prohibited substances. It incorporates by reference the full prohibited substance lists from:

**(a) EU Cosmetics Regulation (EC) No 1223/2009, Annex II** — including all amendments current as of the date of this Standard's publication, including Commission Regulations (EU) 2024/858, 2024/996, and 2025/877. All Annex II substances are prohibited under this Standard regardless of the target market of the product.

**(b) India Cosmetics Rules 2020, Schedule S** — all substances scheduled as prohibited cosmetic ingredients under Notification GSR 126(E) and subsequent amendments. All Schedule S substances are prohibited under this Standard.

**(c) US FDA 21 CFR Part 700** — prohibited cosmetic ingredients under US federal regulation, including mercury compounds, chloroform, bithionol, halogenated salicylanilides, hexachlorophene in OTC cosmetics, vinyl chloride in aerosols, and zirconium-containing complexes in aerosol products. All 21 CFR 700 prohibited substances are incorporated globally under this Standard.

**(d) Health Canada Cosmetic Ingredient Hotlist (prohibited substances)** — substances identified as prohibited in the Health Canada administrative instrument, including current versions as of May 2024 and February 2025 updates. All Hotlist-prohibited substances are incorporated globally under this Standard regardless of whether a product targets the Canadian market.

**(e) Korea MFDS Prohibited Ingredient List** — substances listed as prohibited cosmetic ingredients by the Korean Ministry of Food and Drug Safety, as published in the Ministry's official gazette and cosmetics ingredient list. Incorporated globally under this Standard.

**(f) ASEAN Cosmetic Directive, Annex II (Prohibited Substances)** — the ASEAN Cosmetic Directive, as adopted and given effect by ASEAN member states, Annex II. Incorporated globally under this Standard.

**(g) All CMR Category 1A/1B substances** under the UN Globally Harmonised System (GHS) or EU CLP Regulation 1272/2008. A substance classified as carcinogenic, mutagenic, or toxic to reproduction in Category 1A or 1B is prohibited in any cosmetic product under this Standard. The only exception to this prohibition is where: (i) a specific substance is permitted in a specific cosmetic function at a specific maximum concentration under EU Cosmetics Regulation Annex III, AND (ii) such permission is supported by a current SCCS (Scientific Committee on Consumer Safety) opinion, AND (iii) the specific substance, function, and concentration are documented in the product dossier. The exception is narrow and requires positive evidence on all three grounds.

**(h) All IFRA-prohibited fragrance substances** as identified in the IFRA Standards (49th Amendment, 2022), published by the International Fragrance Association.

**5.3** The TCS analytical detection threshold for prohibited substances is 0.01% w/w. For substances with a threshold effect at very low concentrations (heavy metals, certain carcinogens), lower thresholds apply as specified in Annex I.

**5.4 Unavoidable technical impurities:** Where a prohibited substance appears as an unavoidable technical by-product of the manufacturing process (for example, 1,4-dioxane from ethoxylated ingredients; benzene as a solvent residue), prohibition does not apply provided the brand demonstrates:

(a) All technically feasible purification methods have been applied;

(b) The residual level is below the TCS impurity limit for that substance (Annex I, Table I.3); and

(c) These facts are documented in the product dossier.

## Article 6: Restricted Substances

**6.1** Substances not prohibited under Article 5 may nonetheless be restricted to specific product types, application areas, consumer populations, and maximum concentrations. No product shall contain a restricted substance at a concentration exceeding the TCS limit for the applicable product type and population.

**6.2** TCS maximum concentration limits are set out in Annex II of this Standard (TCS Restricted Substances List). Annex II limits are derived by reviewing all applicable regulatory limits across the source frameworks in Article 33 and applying the most protective (lowest) limit.

**6.3** Where no TCS-specific limit is stated in Annex II for a given substance, the applicable limits are the EU Cosmetics Regulation Annex III limits for the relevant product type, or the limits of any other applicable jurisdiction in Article 5.2 where stricter.

**6.4** Specific restrictions of note:

**(a) Retinoids:** Retinol shall not exceed 0.3% in face leave-on products or 0.05% in body leave-on products. Retinol is prohibited in products marketed for infants and children under 36 months, and in products marketed for use during pregnancy. Retinol-containing products shall carry a label warning that the product is not recommended for use during pregnancy and that use with other retinoid-containing products should be avoided.

**(b) Oxybenzone (Benzophenone-3):** TCS maximum is 4.0% in leave-on products for adults (stricter than the EU Annex VI limit of 6.0%), on the basis of endocrine disruption concern and reef ecosystem impact. Oxybenzone is prohibited in products for infants and children under 36 months, and in facial leave-on products for use during pregnancy.

**(c) Parabens:** Propylparaben and butylparaben individually shall not exceed 0.14% and combined shall not exceed 0.19% (reflecting SCCS precautionary limits for estrogenic activity, stricter than the EU Annex V 0.19%/0.19% combined limit). All four parabens (methyl, ethyl, propyl, butyl) are prohibited in products for infants and children under 36 months.

**(d) Homosalate:** TCS maximum is 7.5% (stricter than the current EU limit pending SCCS review), on the basis of SCCS concern regarding systemic absorption and hormonal activity at higher concentrations.

**(e) Phenoxyethanol:** TCS maximum is 1.0% in general products, 0.4% in products for infants and children under 36 months.

**(f) Methylisothiazolinone (MIT):** Prohibited in all leave-on products. Maximum 0.0015% in rinse-off products only, per SCCS opinion SCCS/1534/14. Prohibited in all products for infants and children under 36 months.

**(g) Formaldehyde:** Prohibited in all leave-on products. Maximum 0.2% in rinse-off products as preservative function only, with mandatory label warning ("Contains formaldehyde") where concentration at or above 0.05%.

**(h) Formaldehyde releasers (as defined in Article 2.13):** Subject to the same leave-on prohibition as formaldehyde itself with respect to infant products. In all other products, each individual releaser is subject to its applicable EU Annex V limit; mandatory disclosure as formaldehyde releasers on the consumer-facing product page is required.

## Article 7: Permitted Preservatives, Colorants, and UV Filters

**7.1 Preservatives:** Only preservatives that appear on the TCS Permitted Preservatives List (derived from EU Cosmetics Regulation Annex V, with TCS-specific limits applied where stricter) are permitted as deliberate preservatives in TCS-certified products. No preservative may be used at a concentration exceeding the applicable TCS limit.

**7.2 Colorants:** Only colorants that appear on the TCS Permitted Colorants List (derived from EU Cosmetics Regulation Annex IV) are permitted in TCS-certified products. Body area restrictions (eyes only, lips only, general use) and concentration limits apply as established by TCS. Colorants not on the permitted list — including coal tar dyes and unlisted synthetic dyes — are prohibited.

**7.3 UV filters:** Only UV filters listed in Annex IV of this Standard are permitted in TCS-certified products carrying sun protection claims. No UV filter may be used at a concentration exceeding the TCS maximum specified in Annex IV. UV filters not listed in Annex IV are prohibited in any product carrying a sun protection claim.

---

## Article 8: Nanomaterials

**8.1** Where an ingredient is used in nano form, the nano form must be separately assessed for safety. Safety data for the conventional (non-nano) form of an ingredient does not automatically apply to the nano form of the same ingredient.

**8.2** The use of nanomaterials that are insoluble or biopersistent is restricted as follows:

(a) **Titanium Dioxide (nano):** Permitted at a maximum of 25% in non-spray formats only. Prohibited in all spray, aerosol, or pump-spray formats where inhalation exposure is possible, consistent with SCCS opinion SCCS/1580/16.

(b) **Zinc Oxide (nano):** Permitted at a maximum of 25% in non-spray formats only. Prohibited in all spray, aerosol, or pump-spray formats, consistent with SCCS opinion SCCS/1489/12.

(c) **Other nanomaterials:** Assessment required on a case-by-case basis against the EU Cosmetics Regulation nano notification requirements and applicable SCCS opinions.

**8.3** Nano forms of any substance on the Annex I Prohibited List are prohibited regardless of particle size distinction.

---

## Article 9: Substances of Very High Concern (SVHC) and Endocrine Disruptors

**9.1** Substances on the ECHA SVHC Candidate List (maintained under REACH Regulation 1907/2006) present in any cosmetic product above 0.1% w/w shall be subject to enhanced scrutiny and shall not be certified without documented consumer risk assessment demonstrating that use in the specific product context is safe. The full list of cosmetic-relevant SVHC Candidate List entries, including their designation basis and TCS position, is provided in **Annex VIII** of this Standard.

**9.2** Where an SVHC substance is also a CMR 1A/1B substance, Article 5.2(g) applies (prohibited, subject to the narrow exception therein).

**9.3** Substances identified as endocrine-disrupting chemicals under EU Regulation 2018/605 (strong evidence criteria) shall be treated with the same level of concern as CMR substances. Their presence in a finished product shall require specific justification in the safety assessment. Specific SVHC entries with ED designation are listed in Annex VIII, Table VIII.A, column "SVHC Basis". The following substance classes identified by ECHA and the EU Commission as priority endocrine disruptors are subject to this Article: bisphenol A (BPA) and its structural analogues; alkylphenols and ethoxylates (Table X.B); phthalates (Annex I, Table I.2, Section B); benzophenone-3 and oxybenzone (Annex VIII, entry 16); cyclic silicones D4 and D5 (Annex VIII, Table VIII.B); and parabens with estrogenic activity (propylparaben, butylparaben, isobutylparaben) at concentrations above those specified in Annex III.

**9.4** Substances under active ECHA endocrine disruptor assessment, or under active restriction proceedings in any jurisdiction covered by Article 33, shall be flagged in the safety assessment and evaluated under the Precautionary Principle (Article 34.3). Assessors shall consult the current ECHA SVHC Candidate List at echa.europa.eu in addition to Annex VIII, as entries are updated bi-annually.

## Article 10: Carcinogens — IARC Classification

**10.1** The International Agency for Research on Cancer (IARC) classification of substances is incorporated into this Standard as a basis for restricting or prohibiting carcinogenic ingredients. The complete list of cosmetic-relevant IARC Group 1, 2A, and 2B substances — with CAS numbers, cosmetic use context, and TCS status — is provided in **Annex VII** of this Standard.

**10.2 IARC Group 1 (Known human carcinogens):** Substances in IARC Group 1 are prohibited in cosmetic products under this Standard. The cosmetic-relevant Group 1 entries are enumerated in **Annex VII, Table VII.A**. Where a substance is in Group 1 and is present in a formulation as an unavoidable impurity, the impurity limits in Annex I, Table I.3 apply.

**10.3 IARC Group 2A (Probable human carcinogens):** Substances in IARC Group 2A present above 0.1% w/w shall not be certified without documented safety assessment demonstrating an acceptable margin of safety at the formulation concentration and pattern of use. The cosmetic-relevant Group 2A entries are enumerated in **Annex VII, Table VII.B**.

**10.4 IARC Group 2B (Possible human carcinogens):** Substances in IARC Group 2B shall be flagged in the safety assessment. Their presence does not automatically preclude certification, but shall be addressed in the safety assessor's conclusion. The cosmetic-relevant Group 2B entries are enumerated in **Annex VII, Table VII.C**.

**10.5** IARC classifications are reviewed annually by the Standards Council. Where IARC upgrades a substance from Group 2B to 2A or Group 1, the Standards Council shall within 30 days issue a determination on whether the substance moves to a more restrictive category under this Standard. Assessors shall consult the current IARC Monographs database at iarc.who.int in addition to Annex VII, as classifications are updated on an ongoing basis.

## Article 11: Contaminants and Unavoidable Impurities

**11.1** Finished products shall not exceed the following TCS limits for contaminants. These limits are set at the most protective level across Health Canada's 2020 Guidance Document on Heavy Metal Impurities in Cosmetics, EU Commission guidance, and WHO/ECHA reference values:

| Contaminant | TCS Maximum | Analytical Method |
|---|---|---|
| Lead | 2 mg/kg | ICP-MS |
| Arsenic | 3 mg/kg | ICP-MS |
| Mercury | 1 mg/kg | ICP-MS or cold vapour AAS |
| Cadmium | 1 mg/kg | ICP-MS |
| Hexavalent chromium | 2 mg/kg | ICP-MS |
| Antimony | 5 mg/kg | ICP-MS |
| 1,4-Dioxane (from ethoxylated ingredients) | 10 mg/kg | GC-MS |
| Benzene (solvent residue) | 2 mg/kg | GC-MS |
| Nitrosamines (NDELA and related) | 0.05 mg/kg (50 ppb) | GC-TEA or LC-MS/MS |
| Acrylamide monomer (from polyacrylamide) | 0.1 mg/kg | LC-MS/MS |
| PAHs (total polycyclic aromatic hydrocarbons) | 2 mg/kg | GC-MS |

**11.2 Nitrosamine risk:** Where a formulation contains a secondary or tertiary amine and a nitrogen-donating ingredient in combination, the brand shall provide nitrosamine testing of the finished product with results below the 0.05 mg/kg limit.

# CHAPTER III — SAFETY ASSESSMENT

---

## Article 12: Safety Assessment Obligation

**12.1** A safety assessment shall be conducted for every product submitted for TCS certification. No product shall be certified without a safety assessment.

**12.2** The safety assessment shall be performed or reviewed and signed by a qualified safety assessor with the following minimum qualifications: a degree in pharmacy, toxicology, medicine, chemistry, biochemistry, or a related discipline; and demonstrable experience in cosmetic product safety assessment.

**12.3** The safety assessment shall cover the product as actually formulated for the market, at the declared concentration of each ingredient. An assessment of a different formulation, a different concentration, or a theoretical product does not meet this requirement.

---

## Article 13: Safety Assessment Content

**13.1** The safety assessment shall contain, as minimum elements:

**(a) Hazard identification** for each ingredient: CMR classification; carcinogenicity (IARC and regulatory classifications); reproductive and developmental toxicity; sensitisation potential; endocrine disruption concern; genotoxicity; systemic organ toxicity; and any other relevant hazard identified in the scientific literature.

**(b) Exposure assessment** as specified in Article 15.

**(c) Margin of Safety calculation** for each ingredient with systemic exposure potential, per Article 14.

**(d) Formulation-level assessment** addressing: ingredient interactions; pH compatibility with actives; preservative system effectiveness; packaging compatibility; and nitrosamine formation risk where applicable.

**(e) Population-specific consideration** for all declared user populations (general adult; infant/child under 36 months; pregnant individuals; sensitive skin).

**(f) Assessor's signed conclusion** that the product is or is not safe for its intended use under normal and reasonably foreseeable conditions of use.

**13.2** Accepted methodology frameworks include the SCCS Notes of Guidance (current revision, SCCS/1647/22) and OECD Test Guidelines. Use of these frameworks is not mandatory — any scientifically rigorous approach meeting the elements of Article 13.1 is acceptable.

---

## Article 14: Margin of Safety

**14.1** The Margin of Safety shall be calculated as: **MoS = NOAEL ÷ SED**

**14.2** Minimum MoS required under this Standard:

| Population | Minimum MoS |
|---|---|
| General adult use | 100 |
| Infants and children under 36 months | 1,000 |
| Products marketed for use during pregnancy | 1,000 (for any ingredient with reproductive or developmental toxicity concern) |

**14.3** Where a product cannot achieve the required MoS for any ingredient at its formulation concentration, the product shall not be certified.

---

## Article 15: Exposure Assessment Methodology

**15.1** The Systemic Exposure Dose (SED) shall be calculated using: product type; area of skin application; frequency of application; amount applied per occasion; dermal absorption rate (measured or estimated by structural analogy); and retention factor for rinse-off products.

**15.2** For leave-on products applied to large body surface areas, cumulative daily exposure across all relevant products of the same type shall be considered in the aggregate.

**15.3** For spray and aerosol products, inhalation exposure shall be assessed in addition to dermal exposure.

**15.4** The SCCS Notes of Guidance (SCCS/1647/22), Tables on default exposure parameters, are the accepted reference values for the exposure assessment. The default parameters from SCCS/1647/22 are reproduced below for reference. Safety assessors shall use these values in the absence of product-specific measured data, and shall document any deviation from defaults with justification.

## Article 16: Good Manufacturing Practice Requirement

**16.1** All products shall be manufactured at a facility operating in conformance with Good Manufacturing Practice (GMP) as defined in ISO 22716:2007 (Cosmetics — Good Manufacturing Practices).

**16.2** Acceptable evidence of GMP compliance, in order of preference:

| Tier | Evidence | Accepted for |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Third-party ISO 22716:2007 certification from an ILAC-accredited certification body (current, valid) | All certification tiers |
| 2 | CDSCO Schedule M-II audit by qualified auditor | India-manufactured products; all tiers |
| 3 | Korea MFDS CGMP certification | Korea-manufactured products; all tiers |
| 4 | ASEAN GMP certificate | ASEAN-manufactured products; all tiers |
| 5 | First-party self-assessment with full documentation package | Entry-level certification only |

**16.3** Products from facilities with no GMP documentation of any kind are ineligible for TCS certification.

---

## Article 17: Quality Management System

**17.1** The manufacturing facility shall operate documented Standard Operating Procedures (SOPs) for all critical processes, including: raw material receipt and testing; batch manufacturing; in-process controls; finished product testing and release; cleaning and hygiene; change control; and deviation and non-conformance handling.

**17.2** A designated qualified or responsible person with documented qualifications shall be identified for the facility's quality management.

**17.3** A documented procedure for handling complaints and adverse events, and for initiating a product recall, shall be maintained.

---

## Article 18: Batch Control and Traceability

**18.1** Every batch of finished product shall be assigned a unique batch or lot number.

**18.2** Forward and backward traceability shall be demonstrated: from each raw material batch through to each finished product batch and its distribution record.

**18.3** Stability data as specified in Article 29 shall be on file for all certified products.

## Article 21: INCI Ingredient List

**21.1** The INCI ingredient list shall include every ingredient present in the finished product, listed in descending order of weight at time of manufacture.

**21.2** Ingredients present at 1% or less may be listed in any order below those present at above 1%, except that colorants must be identified by their Colour Index (CI) number.

**21.3** No ingredient shall be omitted from the INCI list. Deliberate omission of any ingredient is an automatic fail trigger under this Standard (Schedule F of the TCS operational standard).

**21.4** Where a fragrance or perfume is present but individual fragrance ingredients are not required to be listed under applicable law (subject to Article 22), the umbrella INCI term "Parfum" or "Aroma" may be used.

## Article 24: Special Category Label Requirements

# CHAPTER VI — CLAIMS AND ADVERTISING

---

## Article 25: General Claims Principles

**25.1** All consumer-facing claims made by or attributable to the brand about a product shall be:

(a) **Truthful** — factually accurate and not misleading;

(b) **Substantiated** — supported by evidence meeting the minimum levels required under Article 26 before the claim is made;

(c) **Product-specific** — based on evidence from the product as formulated, not merely evidence of the ingredient category in isolation;

(d) **Fair** — not denigrating competitors through exaggerated or unsubstantiated comparative claims;

(e) **Consistent with applicable law** — compliant with the Advertising Standards Council of India (ASCI) Code for all India-targeted claims, and with applicable advertising standards in other target markets.

**25.2** The substantiation obligation is the brand's. Absence of challenge by a regulator does not constitute substantiation.

---

## Article 26: Evidence Standards for Claims

**26.1** TCS recognises the following evidence levels for claim substantiation:

| Level | Description | Sufficient for |
|---|---|---|
| A | Randomised controlled clinical trial; n ≥ 30; peer-reviewed; product-specific | Efficacy claims of any type |
| B | Instrumental measurement study (corneometry, tewameter, mexameter, spectrophotometry, trichoscan, etc.); n ≥ 20; product-specific; blinded assessor | Performance attribute claims (moisture, barrier, oil control) |
| C | Trained panel sensory study; n ≥ 15; product-specific | Sensory and aesthetic claims (texture, absorption, softness) |
| D | Biochemical or cell-based study at relevant concentrations; with clinical correlation | Mechanism claims when supported by product-level data |
| E | Ingredient-level human clinical data at equivalent concentration with bioavailability equivalence demonstrated | Supporting ingredient-specific claims only; shall not independently substantiate product efficacy claims |
| F | In vitro product testing (SPF, UVA, PET, challenge testing) | Safety and SPF claims only; not standalone for efficacy claims |

**26.2** Specific minimum evidence requirements:

| Claim | Minimum required |
|---|---|
| Moisturising / hydrating | Level B |
| Anti-ageing / anti-wrinkle / firming | Level A or B with validated method |
| Brightening / pigmentation / even skin tone | Level A or Level B (spectrophotometric) |
| Sunscreen SPF [value] | In vitro SPF test per ISO 24444; UVA per ISO 24443 |
| Oil control / pore minimising | Level B (sebumeter or equivalent) |
| "Clinically tested" | Level A (clinical trial); if Level B, state method used |
| "Dermatologically tested" | Level B; panel includes minimum 3 dermatologists |
| "Suitable for sensitive skin" | HRIPT (n ≥ 30) with no reactions + Level B skin tolerance data |
| Hair fall reduction | Level B (validated hair count, TrichoScan or equivalent; n ≥ 30) |
| Anti-dandruff | Level A or B demonstrating measurable reduction in scalp flaking |

---

## Article 27: Prohibited Claims

**27.1** The following claims are absolutely prohibited in any medium for TCS-certified products:

| Prohibited claim | Basis |
|---|---|
| "Treats [medical condition]" / "Cures [disease]" / "Prevents [disease]" | Drug claim; requires drug regulatory approval; not a cosmetic claim |
| "Chemical-free" | Scientifically incoherent; all matter is chemical; misleading to consumers |
| "100% natural" for any product containing synthetic preservatives, UV filters, or synthetic fragrance | Factually false |
| "Certified Organic" without COSMOS, NATRUE, or USDA NOP certification from an accredited certifying body | False certification claim |
| "Hypoallergenic" without HRIPT data (minimum n=30) with documented protocol | Unsubstantiated |
| "Dermatologist-approved" based on a single private recommendation | Misleading; implies clinical study |
| "Scientifically proven" without specifying the method and outcome measure | Vague; unsubstantiated |
| "Toxin-free" used as a marketing claim without specific scientific basis | Scientifically imprecise; misleading |
| "Safe for all skin types" without documentary evidence spanning a representative diverse skin population | Unsubstantiated safety claim |
| Claims implying regulatory approval (e.g., "FDA approved," "EU approved") for products not holding such approval | Misleading regarding regulatory status |
| SPF / UVA / broad-spectrum claim without compliant test data | Unsubstantiated; false protection claim |

---

## Article 28: Category-Specific Claim Standards

**28.1 Sunscreens:** SPF claims shall be substantiated by in vitro SPF testing per ISO 24444 and UVA testing per ISO 24443 or equivalent. "Broad-spectrum" claims require UVA/UVB ratio demonstrating adequate UVA protection. Water-resistance claims require ISO 16474 testing.

**28.2 "Clean beauty":** Any product certified under this Standard may use the TCS certification mark in connection with claims of "clean" formulation, subject to the TCS Brand Licence Agreement. Without TCS certification, the term "clean" in a beauty context is not regulated under this Standard; however, it is subject to ASCI Code requirements in India.

**28.3 Ayurvedic claims:** Products claiming Ayurvedic heritage shall use ingredients documented in the Ayurvedic Pharmacopoeia of India (API). Brands shall not conflate Ayurvedic heritage with synthetic active efficacy claims without distinguishing them. If a product is classified as an Ayurvedic drug by AYUSH, a separate AYUSH drug licence is required; TCS cosmetic certification does not apply to that aspect.

## Article 34: The Strictest Standard Principle

**34.1** Where two or more source frameworks listed in Article 33 establish a limit or requirement for the same ingredient, substance, or product characteristic, this Standard applies the most protective requirement.

**34.2** Examples of how this principle is applied:

- Oxybenzone (Benzophenone-3): EU Annex VI permits 6% in leave-on sunscreens. TCS applies 4% on the basis of endocrine disruption concern and precautionary evidence — this is stricter than EU and governs under TCS for all markets.
- Homosalate: EU limit is 10% pending SCCS review. TCS applies 7.5% pre-emptively based on SCCS concern.
- Propylparaben and butylparaben: SCCS precautionary limits lower than EU Annex V; TCS aligns with the more protective SCCS position.
- Heavy metals: TCS applies the most protective limit across Health Canada guidance, EU guidance, and WHO/ECHA values.

**34.3 Precautionary Principle:** Where scientific evidence on an ingredient is incomplete, conflicting, or actively under review, TCS applies precaution and errs on the side of consumer protection. An ingredient under active SCCS review, on the ECHA SVHC Candidate List, or subject to active restriction proceedings in any Article 33 jurisdiction is treated as a concern requiring enhanced safety assessment documentation until the review is complete. Absence of proof of harm does not constitute proof of safety where the hazard profile raises legitimate concern.

## Article 35: Integration of IARC Carcinogen Classifications

**35.1** IARC Monographs are the scientific gold standard for carcinogen evaluation. TCS incorporates IARC classifications as follows:

(a) IARC Group 1 substances: Prohibited in cosmetics (Article 10.2; Annex I, Section A.1);

(b) IARC Group 2A substances: Restricted — detailed safety assessment required, MoS documentation required, concentration above 0.1% not permitted without documented risk assessment (Article 10.3);

(c) IARC Group 2B substances: Flagged in safety assessment; presence does not automatically preclude certification but requires explicit assessor treatment (Article 10.4).

**35.2** The following IARC Group 1 substances are among those prohibited in cosmetics under this Standard: formaldehyde (in leave-on products); benzene (deliberate addition); arsenic compounds (deliberate addition); and others incorporated in Annex I.

**35.3** The Standards Council reviews IARC classification updates annually and incorporates relevant changes into Schedule A and Annex I of this Standard within 30 days of the IARC Monograph update publication.

---

## Article 36: Integration of ECHA SVHC and Endocrine Disruptor Assessments

**36.1** The ECHA SVHC Candidate List is updated by ECHA twice yearly (June and December). The Standards Council tracks these updates and determines within 30 days of each update whether newly added substances require action under this Standard.

**36.2** Substances on the SVHC Candidate List that also meet CMR 1A/1B criteria are incorporated into Annex I (prohibited) consistent with Article 5.2(g).

**36.3** Substances on the SVHC Candidate List that do not meet CMR 1A/1B criteria are subject to the enhanced scrutiny mechanism in Article 9.1.

**36.4** The EU Endocrine Disruptor criteria (Regulation 2018/605) are applied to identify cosmetic ingredients with endocrine-disrupting potential. Substances meeting these criteria under strong evidence are treated as equivalent to CMR substances under this Standard's safety assessment requirements.

## Article 37: Integration of Health Canada NHPID

**37.1** The Natural Health Products Ingredients Database (NHPID) is maintained by Health Canada's Natural and Non-prescription Health Products Directorate. It contains monographs, ingredient safety data, and use conditions for substances used in natural health products (NHPs) in Canada.

**37.2** NHPID is relevant to this Standard in the following specific contexts:

**(a) Botanical raw materials:** The NHPID monographs for plant-derived ingredients contain documented safety data including known contraindications, adverse effect profiles, and maximum use levels. For botanical actives where EU Cosmetics Regulation SCCS opinions are absent or limited, NHPID monograph data is a recognised supplementary source for TCS safety assessments.

**(b) Dual-classified products:** Products that are marketed in Canada and are classified as both cosmetics and natural health products (requiring both a Cosmetic Notification Form and an NHP licence from Health Canada) shall be assessed against both Health Canada cosmetic requirements (Cosmetic Regulations, Hotlist) AND relevant NHPID monograph conditions. Where a NHPID monograph establishes a lower maximum use level or additional contraindication, it applies to any Canadian NHP-classified cosmetic submitted for TCS certification.

**(c) NHPID-prohibited substances for oral-contact and lip products:** For lip products where oral ingestion is a use scenario, NHPID drug-herb interaction data and oral safety data from NHPID monographs shall be consulted as part of the safety assessment, supplementary to the standard cosmetic oral safety assessment.

**(d) Botanical ingredient purity standards:** Where NHPID monographs specify pesticide residue limits, heavy metal limits, or microbial specifications for botanical raw materials more stringent than EU maximum residue limits, those NHPID specifications apply for TCS certification of products targeting the Canadian market.

**37.3** NHPID is publicly accessible at canada.ca/nhpid. The Standards Council reviews NHPID updates annually as part of the standard review cycle.

## Article 38: Integration of Australia AICIS

**38.1** The Australian Industrial Chemicals Introduction Scheme (AICIS), established under the Industrial Chemicals Act 2019 and administered by the Australian Department of Health and Aged Care, regulates the introduction of industrial chemicals — including cosmetic ingredients — into Australia.

**38.2** Relevance to TCS certification:

**(a) Ingredient inventory status:** Every cosmetic ingredient in a product targeting the Australian market shall be listed on the AICIS Inventory or assessed for introduction as an industrial chemical into Australia. AICIS categorises introductions based on risk: certain cosmetic ingredients are designated as low-concern and can be introduced without prior assessment; others require notification or assessment.

**(b) Prohibited and restricted industrial chemicals:** AICIS publishes an Inventory and may issue restrictions or prohibitions on specific industrial chemicals for Australian use. These are incorporated into this Standard's prohibited and restricted substance framework for products targeting Australia.

**(c) Assessment category framework:** Cosmetic ingredients introduced into Australia are categorised by AICIS as: Assessed (previously evaluated); Listed (in the Inventory, assessed as low risk for cosmetic use); Reported (requires notification to AICIS); or Assessed (requires full evaluation). Products targeting Australia shall be formulated using only Assessed or Listed substances unless the brand has obtained the relevant AICIS introduction authorisation.

**38.3 TGA — sunscreens and therapeutic cosmetics:** Products with SPF claims of SPF 4 or greater are classified as therapeutic goods in Australia under the Therapeutic Goods Act 1989 and regulated by the TGA, not AICIS. Such products require listing in the Australian Register of Therapeutic Goods (ARTG). TCS cosmetic certification does not constitute or substitute for TGA registration. TCS may certify the cosmetic ingredient safety aspects of such a product separately from its TGA compliance status, but TCS certification cannot be used to represent Australian market authorisation for SPF-claiming products.

**38.4** The Standards Council maintains awareness of AICIS assessment outcomes and Inventory updates. Where AICIS issues a finding that a cosmetic ingredient presents unacceptable risk, the Standards Council shall determine within 30 days whether that ingredient should move to TCS Annex I (prohibited) or Annex II (restricted) regardless of other market frameworks.

## Article 39: Standard Maintenance and Amendments

**39.1** This Standard shall be reviewed annually by the TCS Standards Council.

**39.2 Emergency amendments:** Where a regulatory body listed in Article 33 issues a prohibition on a cosmetic ingredient, the Standards Council may add that substance to Annex I with immediate effect, without waiting for the annual review cycle. Brands with certified products containing the substance shall be notified within 5 business days and shall have a defined period (specified in the amendment notice, minimum 90 days) to reformulate before certification is suspended.

**39.3** The annual review shall consider: new SCCS opinions; new IARC classifications; new ECHA SVHC additions; amendments to any framework in Article 33; post-market safety signals; and any new scientific literature materially changing the safety profile of a substance in any Annex.

**39.4** All amendments are version-controlled. Version history is publicly available on the TCS Registry.

## ANNEX VII — IARC CARCINOGEN CLASSIFICATIONS: COSMETIC-RELEVANT SUBSTANCES

**Source:** International Agency for Research on Cancer (IARC) Monographs on the Evaluation of Carcinogenic Risks to Humans. Classifications current as of the 2024 IARC Monographs database.

**Classification definitions:**
- **Group 1:** Carcinogenic to humans — sufficient evidence in humans
- **Group 2A:** Probably carcinogenic to humans — limited evidence in humans, sufficient evidence in animals
- **Group 2B:** Possibly carcinogenic to humans — limited evidence in humans
- **Group 3:** Not classifiable as to carcinogenicity (listed below only where relevant to assessment of related Group 1/2A substances)

**TCS policy:** Group 1 substances are prohibited in finished cosmetics (see Annex I, Table I.2, Section F and cross-references). Group 2A substances require case-by-case restriction; they are prohibited unless a specific safe-use limit is established by SCCS, IFRA, or another recognised safety authority and that limit is respected. Group 2B substances are subject to enhanced safety assessment and precautionary limits.

### Table X.C — Korea MFDS Positive List Principles for Quasi-Drug / Cosmetic Boundary

Certain ingredients are freely permitted in cosmetics in EU but regulated as quasi-drugs or pharmaceuticals in Korea (and some ASEAN states). The TCS framework applies the following categorisation boundary rules:

| Ingredient Category | Korea Classification Threshold | TCS Rule |
|---|---|---|
| Retinol (Vitamin A alcohol) | >2,500 IU/g (≈0.08% retinol) in leave-on = quasi-drug in Korea | TCS: ≤0.3% retinol in leave-on products; with safety data per SCCS/1577/16 |
| Retinyl Palmitate | No Korea quasi-drug threshold currently; cosmetic permitted | TCS: ≤1.0% retinyl palmitate in leave-on |
| Niacinamide | >5% = quasi-drug in Korea | TCS: ≤5% in any product (adopts strictest across markets) |
| Alpha-Arbutin | >2% = restricted in Singapore and some ASEAN states | TCS: ≤2% maximum |
| Tranexamic Acid | >2% = possible quasi-drug in Korea | TCS: ≤2% in leave-on skin care |
| Glycolic Acid | Concentration-dependent; >10% at pH <3.5 = borderline in Korea | TCS: ≤10% AHA total; pH must be ≥3.5 in consumer products |
| Salicylic Acid | >0.5% = quasi-drug in Korea for acne indication | TCS: ≤2% rinse-off (shampoo/cleanser); ≤0.5% leave-on unless product makes no drug claims |

## ANNEX XI — REACH ANNEX XVII RESTRICTIONS APPLICABLE TO COSMETICS

**Source:** Regulation (EC) No 1907/2006 (REACH) Annex XVII — Restrictions on the manufacture, placing on the market, and use of certain dangerous substances, mixtures, and articles. This Annex lists REACH Annex XVII entries specifically relevant to cosmetic products or ingredients.

**Note on scope:** REACH Annex XVII applies to all articles and substances placed on the EU market, including cosmetic products. REACH restrictions may apply to cosmetics independently of EU Cosmetics Regulation 1223/2009 Annex II restrictions. Where both frameworks restrict a substance, the stricter limit applies.
